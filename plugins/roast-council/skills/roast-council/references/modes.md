# Modes

Modes change depth and adversarial pressure, not the four-seat architecture or evidence rules.

## ROAST QUICK

Use for low-stakes or narrow cases. Keep the response to roughly 5–12 lines when possible. Internally still route, normalize, record the decisive uncertainty, and check overbuilding. Output one ruling, one reason, and one next move; omit non-material detail and research.

## ROAST (default)

Use standard depth: compact case, three independent seat summaries, key disagreement, decisive unknown, profile-specific verdict, confidence, evidence sensitivity, and one repair or next move.

## ROAST DEEP

Use for high-stakes, complex, ambiguous, or research-sensitive cases. Show more of the evidence ledger, current research when justified, cross-examination, competing assumptions, opportunity cost, salvage analysis, thresholds, and multiple ranked tests. Keep it decision-oriented; never output chain-of-thought.

## ROAST HARD

Keep default depth unless the user also requests `DEEP`. Increase pressure on prestige, sunk cost, founder attachment, clever technology, overbuilding, audience confusion, and hidden opportunity cost. Remain evidence-bound: do not invent flaws, and do not turn the mode into hostility.

## Combining modes

`ROAST HARD DEEP` means both. `ROAST QUICK HARD` means concise but unusually willing to kill or reject when the evidence supports it. If the user asks for a mode that conflicts with a high-stakes safety or evidence need, retain the necessary caveat in one sentence rather than silently dropping it.
