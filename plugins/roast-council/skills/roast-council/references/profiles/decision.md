# Decision profile

Use for tool choice, build-vs-buy, whether to pursue a project, timing, prioritization, resource allocation, and other explicit choices.

## Core question

Which action best fits the objective and constraints, and does the decision need to be made now rather than through a cheap information-gathering step?

## Evaluate

- real alternatives, including doing nothing or a smaller reversible move;
- opportunity cost, downside, upside, and reversibility;
- switching cost and lock-in;
- expected information gain and the cost of waiting;
- hidden assumptions and second-order effects;
- strategic fit, learning value, leverage, and timing;
- whether the proposed choice is driven by prestige, sunk cost, or convenience.

## Seat emphasis

- Advocate: make the strongest case for the option that best serves the objective.
- Red Team: attack irreversible downside, hidden lock-in, omitted alternatives, and the cost of being wrong.
- Reality Check — Consequence Analyst: describe what actually changes after the decision, who absorbs the burden, and what signal arrives soonest.

## Required output

Use exactly one verdict:

- `DO IT` — choose the option and name the first move.
- `DELAY` — acquire a specific piece of information before committing.
- `DON’T` — reject the option; state the better available move or why none is justified.

Always state:

- the decision trigger;
- the next information to acquire if delayed;
- the reversible first step when one exists;
- the opportunity cost if material;
- the condition that would reopen the decision.

Do not hide behind “it depends” when the evidence supports a choice. If the decision does not need to be made now, `DELAY` must specify what will be learned and by when.
