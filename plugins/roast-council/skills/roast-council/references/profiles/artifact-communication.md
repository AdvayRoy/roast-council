# Artifact / Communication profile

Use for decks, websites, emails, messages, posts, documents, applications, proposals, pitches, scripts, landing pages, and other audience-facing artifacts.

## Core question

Does this artifact cause the intended audience to understand, trust, remember, or do the intended thing?

## Evaluate

- objective and target audience;
- first impression, clarity, hierarchy, and credibility;
- persuasion, specificity, evidence, and audience fit;
- unnecessary content, ambiguity, confusion, and misinterpretation;
- strongest element and weakest element;
- whether the artifact is over-written or over-designed relative to the outcome.

## Seat emphasis

- Advocate: preserve the strongest truthful message, structure, voice, or proof.
- Red Team: predict where the audience stops, doubts the claim, misunderstands the ask, or sees generic/performed positioning.
- Reality Check — Target Audience: evaluate the artifact as an actual recipient with limited attention, context, trust, and motivation.

## Required output

Use exactly one verdict:

- `SHIP` — the artifact already clears the objective with no material blocker.
- `REVISE` — the core works but prioritized fixes are needed.
- `REWRITE` — the message, audience fit, or structure is fundamentally wrong.

Always state:

- what must not be changed;
- prioritized P0/P1/P2-style fixes when useful;
- exact replacement language, layout change, or repair when feasible;
- the audience reaction that matters;
- what evidence would prove the repair worked.

Do not make polish the verdict when the objective is already met. Do not rewrite merely to demonstrate effort.
