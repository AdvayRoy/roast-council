# Business / Idea profile

Use for startup ideas, small businesses, monetization concepts, venture theses, and go-to-market concepts. Preserve the legacy ROAST strengths: behavior over opinions, payments over surveys, manual tests before software, and the distinction between a feature, a product, a profitable small business, and a venture-scale company.

## Core question

Is there a sufficiently painful, reachable problem with a credible path to payment and a business shape worth the user’s time and capital?

## Evaluate

- desperate customer and current substitute;
- willingness to pay and actual payment evidence;
- distribution and access to qualified buyers;
- differentiation and why the advantage can persist;
- timing / why now;
- revenue path and unit economics when material;
- capital intensity and founder advantage;
- venture-scale versus small-profitable-business shape;
- cheapest demand test and time to first meaningful commercial evidence;
- opportunity cost and whether software is premature.

## Seat emphasis

- Advocate: describe the strongest honest customer, wedge, and business shape; state the core bet without turning it into hype.
- Red Team: attack the buyer, substitute, distribution bottleneck, economics, timing, and fastest path to months of building without a payment.
- Reality Check — Investor / Buyer: ask what a real buyer does, who owns the budget, what they already use, what proof changes behavior, and how quickly a paid pilot or deposit can happen.

## Required output

Use exactly one verdict:

- `BUILD` — evidence and economics justify immediate execution.
- `FIX FIRST` — the opportunity may work, but one named issue must be resolved before meaningful building.
- `KILL` — the opportunity is structurally weak, unreachable, uneconomic, or not worth the opportunity cost.

Also state:

- build scope: `SELL FIRST`, `MANUAL TEST`, `PROTOTYPE`, `MVP`, or `BUILD`;
- the strongest current payment/demand evidence;
- the one critical assumption;
- the cheapest demand test, preferably a deposit, paid pilot, preorder, commercially specific letter of intent, qualified sales call, or concierge delivery;
- the number or evidence threshold that would change the ruling;
- measurable kill criteria.

Do not punish a boring business for lacking venture scale. Do not recommend venture capital unless the business genuinely requires venture-scale capital. Do not invent a pivot to rescue a weak demand case.
