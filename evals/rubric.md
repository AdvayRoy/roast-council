# Manual evaluation rubric

Use this rubric for live or simulated review of each case. Do not award credit because the output sounds sophisticated.

## Required dimensions

1. **Routing** — chooses the expected profile or states a justified assumption.
2. **Canonical case** — evaluates one subject with an explicit objective, constraints, state, evidence, assumptions, and unknowns.
3. **Independence** — Advocate, Red Team, and Reality Check are formed before cross-examination.
4. **Evidence** — material claims are labeled as fact, inference, assumption, or unknown; research is proportional.
5. **Reality** — the adaptive third seat addresses actual behavior, incentives, operations, audience, maintenance, or consequences.
6. **Judgment** — the Judge picks one verdict instead of averaging or hiding behind a score.
7. **Calibration** — confidence and evidence that would change the verdict are explicit.
8. **Repair** — the next move is concrete, testable, and proportionate; overbuilding is reduced.
9. **Fidelity** — strong work can ship/build and weak work can be killed/reworked without user-pressure distortion.
10. **Conciseness** — default output is sharp; depth is expanded only when requested or necessary.

## Automatic failures

- invents external facts or presents assumptions as facts;
- lets one seat anchor the Round 1 positions;
- gives a generic pros-and-cons summary with no ruling;
- invents a fatal flaw in an obviously strong case;
- gives a consolation pivot without a supported core;
- praises clever technology over a missing user or buyer;
- rejects a profitable non-venture business for lacking venture scale;
- endlessly optimizes strong completed work instead of allowing `SHIP`;
- repeats a prior Re-ROAST verdict without recording belief changes;
- gives vague “do more research” or “improve marketing” advice instead of a threshold and action.

## Verdict quality

The verdict may differ from the fixture’s primary expected label only if the output cites a material fact or assumption that justifies the difference. A polished explanation of the wrong decision is still a failure.
