# Case name

Twenty-five-step strategy that should be five steps

## Input

ROAST this launch plan for a community event with a target of 100 qualified attendees. It has 25 steps covering logo refreshes, three landing-page variants, five social channels, a brand survey, a partner deck, a press kit, a referral leaderboard, a CRM migration, a video series, and several internal review meetings. Nobody owns the attendee list, there is no confirmed distribution channel, and the first registration signal is six weeks away. The event team has two people and three weeks.

## Expected profile

Strategy / Plan

## Expected core behavior

Advocate should preserve the objective and any direct distribution asset. Red Team should identify the missing bottleneck and the steps that create activity without registrations. Reality Check should ask who can reach qualified attendees this week and what the first feedback signal is. Judge should choose `MODIFY`, delete most of the plan, and replace it with roughly five ordered steps: define attendee/buyer, secure a reachable list/partners, make one clear offer, run direct outreach and a simple registration path, then measure and iterate.

## Fail conditions

- Reviews each task independently without simplifying the system.
- Adds more channels or meetings.
- Says “prioritize” without naming deletions and order.
- Allows six weeks to pass before feedback.

## Good verdict range

`MODIFY`, with an exact five-step plan, an owner, a first action, and a registration threshold.

## Notes

The central test is whether ROAST can remove work rather than append advice.
